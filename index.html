    03 / The context

    Why this
    matters.

    Green space is not a decorative layer. It is part of the city’s everyday infrastructure: social, ecological and unevenly distributed.

    01

    Shared ground

    Parks and open spaces are among the few places in a city that are not organised around spending. Their distribution shapes where people can pause, play, meet and notice seasonal change.

    02

    Spatial clues

    A map makes the pattern visible. It can prompt better questions about density, neighbourhood difference and the relationship between built-up land and open space.

    03

    A measured start

    This prototype shows location and recorded area. It does not measure walking time, quality, safety or who uses each place.

    04 / Notes

    Read the
    small print.

    What you are seeing. The map is an exploratory client-side visualisation built with HTML, CSS, JavaScript and Leaflet. The green-space layer is loaded from a local GeoJSON file and can be filtered by name and category.

    What it cannot tell you. A mapped location is not the same as an accessible location. The current view does not account for entrances, paths, slope, maintenance, perceived safety or travel time.

    Next steps. A fuller study could add population data, street-network travel times, accessibility audits and time-based change. It could also compare provision with local environmental and social indicators.
